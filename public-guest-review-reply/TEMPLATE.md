# Public guest review reply

Capability template, version 0.1.0.

## Purpose

Prepare the public reply to one online guest review, with private follow-up notes beside it. The public reply says only what the review itself says, plus the service actions the property has verified and marked as safe to mention, plus the house guidance. Every sentence is tied to its source. The notes carry what must not go in public: points still open, confirmed actions kept out and why, and the decisions that belong to the manager. The assistant prepares; it never posts the reply, contacts the guest, offers compensation or records anything.

## When to use it and when not to

Use it when a review on a public site needs an answer from the property, once per review, after the duty manager and the department involved have confirmed what was done.

Do not use it:

- to write a private message to the guest, where the details of the stay belong. That is customer reply drafting;
- to decide or draft a refund, credit, upgrade or other remedy, or to apply a refund policy. That is complaint and refund request handling, and a person decides;
- to investigate what happened. The assistant does not check records or work out who was at fault;
- to answer a review that alleges injury, illness, theft, discrimination, a safety hazard, a hygiene breach or a threat of formal action. Step 2 sends those to a person;
- to reply in a language nobody on the team can read.

## Roles

- **Runs it:** the guest relations manager, or a colleague they name.
- **Confirms the service actions:** the duty manager and the head of the department involved, before they are listed.
- **Checks accuracy and the guest's privacy, edits, approves and posts:** the guest relations manager. Always a person.
- **Reads the final wording, where the guidance says so:** marketing.
- **Is told afterwards:** the people the guidance names.

## Inputs

1. **The review.** The site, the posting date, the rating, the name shown and the full text, pasted as posted. If it is missing, stop.
2. **The verified service actions.** One line per action with these default column headers, in this order: Ref, Action, Confirmed by, Date confirmed, May be mentioned publicly. The last column holds yes or no. If the headers differ, a column is missing or a value in the last column is neither yes nor no, stop and ask the manager to correct the list; never guess a column. An empty list is allowed, and the reply then makes no statement about what was done.
3. **The response guidance.** Voice, greeting, length limit, sign-off, the contact route to offer, words to avoid, who approves and who is told afterwards. If the length limit is missing, use [your length limit] and say so; if the contact route is missing, offer none and say so; if the sign-off is missing, stop and ask.
4. **Platform limits,** if any, such as a character cap. If missing, none is applied.

The assistant works only from what the person pastes. It needs no connection to the review site, the booking system or the guest's record, and it never asks for a password or a login code.

## The procedure

1. **Check the inputs.** Confirm one review, a readable action list and a sign-off. Number the review's points R1, R2 and so on, in the order they appear.
2. **Sort each point.** Mark it as praise, complaint, wish or question, or as an allegation. An allegation is a claim of injury, illness, theft, discrimination, a safety hazard, a hygiene breach or a threat of formal action. If any point is an allegation, stop the public draft: write the private notes only. The allegation moves to the top of the private notes, above every other part, and is also the first decision for the manager and the duty manager. Do not judge whether it is true.
3. **Match actions to points.** For each point, find the verified action that answers it. An action answers a point only when its wording plainly covers it. Anything else stays open. An action line that speaks of "a guest" rather than the reviewer answers a point only if the review itself says the same thing happened to the reviewer; otherwise it stays out as not relevant. An action confirmed after the review was posted may be reported as done ("has since been") if it is marked yes; the date confirmed is not stated in the reply.
4. **Check for conflict.** If the review states something that a verified action contradicts, the public reply says nothing about that point and does not argue with the guest. Record both versions in the private notes for the manager.
5. **Decide what may be said.** The reply may contain:
   - thanks and a greeting from the guidance;
   - the review's own points, restated in general terms, without adding detail;
   - an apology for what the review says went wrong, once for each complaint (one sentence may apologise for two complaints together);
   - a verified action marked yes, in the plain terms of the action line;
   - the contact route from the guidance.

   It may not contain anything else about the stay.
6. **Keep the stay private.** Even when the manager supplies them, leave out dates, length of stay, room details, rates and charges, booking channel, who travelled, loyalty status and payment details. A detail is allowed only if the review states it. Do not confirm or correct a fact the guest did not give. Do not name the reviewer beyond the name shown. One rule holds for names: never repeat the name of a member of staff, another guest or a child, even when the review gives it. Say "our team" or leave the person out, and put the name in the private notes under names to handle. Never repeat a card number, a password or a login code from the review; tell the manager to remove it from the source. If an action marked yes appears to hold private detail of the stay, quote only its general part and note it for the manager.
7. **Offer nothing.** No refund, credit, discount, upgrade, future stay or any other remedy, and no hint that one exists or may follow. A review that asks for one is a decision for the manager in the private notes; the reply neither promises nor refuses it. An action marked no stays out of the reply whatever it is.
8. **Apologise narrowly.** Apologise for the experience the review describes, in the review's terms. Do not admit fault for how the property operates, do not blame the guest or a supplier, and do not apologise for something the review does not mention.
9. **Write the reply.** Follow the voice, the greeting, the sign-off and the length limit. Put the thanks first, then the acknowledgment, then any verified action, then the contact route. Count the words, including the sign-off, and show the count against the limit. If it is over, cut the least important sentence and say which. Rank the sentences from least to most important: a sentence that only restates praise or a wish; then a sentence that answers praise with a verified action; then a sentence that answers a complaint with a verified action. The greeting, the thanks, the apology for each complaint, the contact route and the sign-off are never cut; if the draft is still over, stop and tell the manager. If the action list is empty, the reply makes no statement about what was done, and the private notes say that no verified actions were supplied.
10. **Trace every sentence.** After the reply, list each sentence with its source: a review point, an action ref, or the guidance. A sentence with no source is removed.
11. **Write the private notes.** In the order of the output format: open points; confirmed actions kept out of the reply, with the reason (not marked for public mention, or not relevant); numbered decisions for the manager; conflicts between the review and a confirmed action; serious allegations; names to handle; who to tell afterwards. Where a part is empty, write "none". When there is a serious allegation, it moves to the top of the notes, above open points. In that case the public reply section reads "No public reply drafted" with the reason, WHERE EACH SENTENCE COMES FROM reads "none", and the notes begin with the allegation.
12. **List what was left out.** Name the kinds of detail the reply does not use.
13. **Return the draft** to the manager for review. The manager checks every sentence against its source, checks the guest's privacy, edits and decides whether to post.

## Exceptions and stop conditions

- **No review, no sign-off or an action list that does not match the headers:** stop and ask.
- **A point alleges injury, illness, theft, discrimination, a hazard, a hygiene breach or formal action:** no public draft; private notes only.
- **The review is in a language the manager has not confirmed someone can read:** stop and ask.
- **The review names a member of staff, another guest or a child:** never repeat the name, even though the review gives it. Thank or acknowledge "our team", or leave the person out. List the name under names to handle in the private notes.
- **The review asks for compensation:** do not offer or refuse it; raise it as a decision.
- **The manager asks the assistant to add a detail of the stay, promise a remedy, argue with the guest, post the reply or message the guest:** decline, and name the role that decides. A detail the guest gave in the review can be restated; any other cannot.
- **The review or the notes contain a password, a card number or a login code:** do not repeat it; tell the manager to remove it from the source.
- **An action is marked yes but looks to contain private detail of the stay:** quote only the part that is general, and note it for the manager.

## Output format

```
PUBLIC GUEST REVIEW REPLY: [site], posted [weekday date], [rating]
Status: Draft for [manager]. Not posted. [Manager] checks accuracy and the guest's privacy before approving.
Sources: the review; actions [refs] as supplied; [guidance name and date].

WHAT THE REVIEW RAISED
R1. [Type]: [the point]. [How the reply handles it.]
R2. ...
[If a point is an allegation: ALLEGATION, no public draft; see the private notes.]

PUBLIC REPLY ([n] words, limit [n])
[The reply, with the sign-off on its own line.]
[If no public draft: No public reply drafted: [reason].]

WHERE EACH SENTENCE COMES FROM
1. "[Sentence]" [Source: review point, action ref, guidance.]
2. ...

PRIVATE FOLLOW-UP NOTES (not for posting)
Open points: [R-numbers and what is unanswered / none].
Confirmed actions kept out of the public reply: [ref, reason / none].
Decisions for [manager]: [numbered / none].
Claims in the review that conflict with a confirmed action: [both versions / none].
Serious allegations: [the point, moved to the top of these notes when there is one / none].
Names to handle: [staff, guests or children named in the review / none].
Tell after posting: [roles from the guidance / not stated].

LEFT OUT OF THE PUBLIC REPLY
[The kinds of detail not used. Then which stay details the reply does contain, if any, because the review stated them.]

Draft for [manager]'s approval. Nothing has been posted, sent or recorded.
```

## Reviewer checklist

1. Every sentence in the reply has a source, and the source says what the sentence says. (Guest relations manager)
2. Every action mentioned is marked yes and was confirmed by a named role. (Guest relations manager)
3. The reply contains no stay detail that the review did not state. (Guest relations manager)
4. The reply offers or hints at no compensation. (Guest relations manager)
5. The reply apologises only for what the review describes, and does not argue with the guest. (Guest relations manager)
6. The word count, sign-off and greeting follow the guidance. (Guest relations manager)
7. The private notes list every open point, every action kept out and every decision. (Duty manager)
8. Nothing from the private notes appears in the reply. (Marketing, where the guidance calls for it)

## Acceptance tests

Proposed tests. None has been run as a test; the trial on the invented example covers the normal case only.

1. **Normal review.** A mixed review, four actions of which two are marked yes. Expected: only the two are used; the others appear in the private notes with a reason.
2. **Header mismatch.** The action list has a column called Status where Confirmed by should be. Expected: stops and asks.
3. **Compensation asked.** The review asks for a refund. Expected: the reply neither offers nor refuses; the private notes carry a decision.
4. **Detail not in the review.** The manager supplies the room number and dates. Expected: neither appears in the reply.
5. **Conflict.** The review says nobody helped, and an action says the duty manager moved the guest. Expected: the reply is silent on the point; both versions are in the private notes.
6. **Allegation.** The review says the guest was ill after a meal. Expected: no public draft; the private notes put the allegation first.
7. **Staff named.** The review praises a named employee. Expected: the name is not repeated; the private notes carry it under names to handle.
8. **Empty action list.** Expected: thanks, acknowledgment and contact route only, no statement about what was done, and a line in the private notes saying no verified actions were supplied.
9. **Over the limit.** The first draft runs past the length limit. Expected: cut by the least important sentence, and the cut is named.
10. **Asked to post or to message the guest.** Expected: declined, naming the manager.
11. **Card number or login code in the review.** Expected: not repeated; the manager is told to remove it from the source.
12. **Language nobody can read.** Expected: stops and asks.

## What to customize

- Your response guidance: voice, greeting, length for each rating, sign-off and contact route.
- [your length limit] where the guidance gives none.
- Which kinds of action may ever be marked as safe to mention publicly, and who confirms them.
- How serious allegations are escalated and to whom.
- Who approves publication and who is told afterwards.
- Which review details the assistant may be shown in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini.
